# UChicago Claude Enterprise Adoption: Baseline Narrative (through September 15, 2026)

*This narrative covers the period from the initial faculty/staff rollout through September 14, 2026 — the day before student access opened broadly. All figures are sourced from the dashboard data files; no values are fabricated. Because this dashboard tracks only publicly shared projects and plugin/connector usage visible in the organizational interface, it represents a deliberate-visibility subset of total activity. Per-project and per-creator detail (project titles, descriptions, and creators) is kept private (UChicago-only); only aggregate project figures are published here.*

---

## Early Adoption: A Faculty and Staff Foundation

From the initial rollout through mid-September 2026, Claude Enterprise adoption at the University of Chicago was concentrated among faculty, staff, and select administrators. The September 17 snapshot — taken two days after students gained access — captures a baseline shaped almost entirely by this earlier cohort. Sixty-six publicly shared projects were observed, with Research (22 projects) and Administrative Operations (10 projects) as the leading categories. Only aggregate, category-level figures are published; per-creator detail is kept private. The distribution suggests that early adopters were disproportionately researchers and administrative professionals exploring AI-assisted workflows, which aligns with typical faculty-first enterprise rollout patterns at research universities.

## Connector Adoption: Deliberate and Narrow

Of the 40 plugins and connectors tracked in the organizational catalog, only 11 showed measurable adoption (at least one user in the preceding 30 days) at the time of the first snapshot. The top two connectors — canvas-design and learn — each registered 7 users; doc-coauthoring followed at 4. The remaining adopted connectors had 2–3 users each. Twenty-nine connectors recorded zero organizational users. This pattern is consistent with a small early-adopter population exploring a limited set of relevant tools rather than broad organizational uptake, and should not be interpreted as low enthusiasm — the same connectors with zero adoption today may see rapid growth as the student population onboards.

## The Signal That Matters: IT Services Knowledge-Base Demand

The most consequential indicator in the pre-student-access period is not project or connector counts, but the ServiceNow knowledge-base page views. The article "Request Access to Claude Enterprise" (KB06004829) had accumulated **21,500 lifetime views** by September 17 — a figure that dwarfs the count of active users visible through the organizational interface. This strongly suggests that interest in the tool significantly preceded or exceeded the population with active access, and that demand was largely unmet prior to the broader rollout. The download guide (KB06004835, 1,043 views) and personal-account setup article (KB06004851, 472 views) add further context: users were actively seeking onboarding information, not merely discovering the tool passively.

## What the Data Does Not Show

This dashboard does not capture the volume of individual Claude conversations, the depth of use within existing projects, or adoption by users who have not created or joined a public project. A researcher who uses Claude extensively for private drafts, data analysis, or literature review would not appear in the project count. The figures here are best understood as a floor — a minimum-visibility signal — rather than a ceiling or a complete picture of engagement.

## Outlook

The opening of student access on September 15, 2026 represents the most significant expansion of the potential user base since the program launched. Subsequent daily snapshots will show whether the project count, connector adoption, and knowledge-base page views accelerate in the weeks that follow. The page-view trajectory on KB06004829 in particular will be an early indicator of whether demand is being converted into active use.
